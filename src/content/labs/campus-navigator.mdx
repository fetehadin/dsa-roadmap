## The task

New students get lost. Build a console navigator for campus:

1. **Buildings** live in a **BST keyed by name**, so lookups are O(log n) on a balanced tree, and an inorder walk lists them A to Z for free.
2. **Paths** between buildings form an **undirected graph**, stored as an **adjacency list**.
3. **BFS** gives the route with the fewest hops. **DFS** tells you everything reachable.
4. Results are **sorted**: nearest buildings with **merge sort** (stable, by hops then name) and reachable names with **quick sort**.

## Input format

```text
n
name1 name2 … nameN         (single-word names)
m
a b                         (m lines: a path between buildings a and b)
ROUTE a b | NEAREST a | REACH a     (commands until end of input)
```

Visit neighbours in **alphabetical order** so your output matches the sample.

## Questions to answer in your report

- Why is the inorder traversal of the BST sorted?
- What is the time complexity of ROUTE in terms of V and E?
- The Clinic has no paths. What do BFS and DFS do with it, and why?
- Would an adjacency matrix be a better choice here? Count the cells vs the list entries.
