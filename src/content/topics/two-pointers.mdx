Two people search a **sorted bookshelf** for two books whose prices add up to 15 birr. One starts at the cheap end, the other at the expensive end. Too expensive? The person at the expensive end steps in. Too cheap? The person at the cheap end steps in. They never need to go back.

### Why it works

The array is sorted. If `a[l] + a[r]` is **too small**, then `a[l]` paired with anything left of `r` is even smaller, so `a[l]` is useless: move `l` right. If it is **too big**, `a[r]` is useless: move `r` left. Every step discards one item, so the search is **O(n)** instead of checking all **n²** pairs.

### Two flavours

- **Opposite ends** (`l = 0`, `r = n − 1`, move inward): pair sums, reversing, palindromes, container with most water.
- **Same direction** (a slow write pointer and a fast read pointer): removing duplicates in place, moving zeros. Fast & slow pointers on linked lists are the same idea.
