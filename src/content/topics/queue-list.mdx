Picture a **conga line**. Each dancer holds the shoulders of the dancer in front. New dancers join at the back; the leader leaves from the front.

A linked-list queue keeps **two pointers**:

- **`front`** → the head node. Dequeue removes it: O(1).
- **`rear`** → the last node. Enqueue links a new node after it: `rear->next = n; rear = n;`, O(1).

Why not the other way round? Removing the **tail** of a singly linked list needs the node **before** it, and finding that node means walking the whole list.

### The two edge cases

1. **Enqueue into an empty queue**: there is no `rear->next` to set. Point **both** `front` and `rear` at the new node.
2. **Dequeue the last node**: `front` becomes `nullptr`. Set **`rear = nullptr`** too, or it will dangle.

There is no `isFull()`, and no wasted slots.
