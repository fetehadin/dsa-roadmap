Removing someone from an **org chart**. If they manage nobody, just remove them. If they manage one person, that person reports to their old boss. If they manage two teams, you need a replacement who fits between both teams. That is the whole idea.

First **search** for the node like normal. Then:

### Case 1: a leaf (no children)

Just remove it. The parent's pointer becomes `nullptr`.

### Case 2: one child

**Bypass** it: the parent points directly to the node's only child. The whole subtree moves up one level and the BST property still holds.

### Case 3: two children

You cannot bypass a node with two children. Instead, find a value that can **sit in its place**: bigger than everything on the left, smaller than everything on the right. That is the **inorder successor**, the **smallest value in the right subtree** (go right once, then left until you can't).

1. Copy the successor's value into the node.
2. Delete the successor from the right subtree. It has no left child, so this is case 1 or 2.

(Using the **inorder predecessor**, the largest on the left, also works. Both are correct; pick one and be consistent.)

### Why the code is short

The recursive version returns the **new root of each subtree**. Cases 1 and 2 merge into one: "if there is no left child, return the right child (which may be `nullptr`)".
